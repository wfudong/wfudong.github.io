Practice 4: Directional Derivatives, Chain Rule, and Optimization

Estimated time: 45–60 minutes

Focus: Based on Lecture 4: gradient-direction relations, chain rule, Jacobian linearization, and constrained/unconstrained extrema.

What You'll Practice

  • Directional derivatives with unit vectors
  • Steepest increase and gradient norm
  • Chain rule for one-parameter and two-parameter maps
  • Jacobian entries and linear approximation
  • Second derivative test and Lagrange multipliers

  1. Exercise 1: Directional Derivative

    For $f(x,y)=x^2y+y^3$, at $(1,1)$ along $u=(3/5,4/5)$, $D_u f(1,1)=$

    Hint & Solution

    Hint: Compute $\nabla f=(2xy,\;x^2+3y^2)$ then dot with $u$.

    Solution: $\nabla f(1,1)=(2,4)$, so $D_u f=(2,4)\cdot(3/5,4/5)=22/5$.

  2. Exercise 2: Unit-Vector Requirement

    Is the statement true: “In $D_u f=\nabla f\cdot u$, vector $u$ must be unit length.”

    Hint & Solution

    Hint: Formula $D_u f=\nabla f\cdot u$ assumes unit direction.

    Solution: True. If direction vector is not unit, normalize first.

  3. Exercise 3: Steepest Increase

    For $f(x,y)=x^2+y^2$ at $(1,2)$, the maximum directional derivative equals:

    Hint & Solution

    Hint: Maximum directional derivative is $\|\nabla f\|$.

    Solution: $\nabla f=(2x,2y)$, at $(1,2)$: $(2,4)$, norm $=2\sqrt{5}$.

  4. Exercise 4: Chain Rule (One Parameter)

    Let $f(x,y)=x^2y$, $x=t^2$, $y=e^t$. Compute $\dfrac{dz}{dt}$ at $t=0$.

    Hint & Solution

    Hint: Use lecture formula $\frac{dz}{dt}=(4t^3+t^4)e^t$.

    Solution: At $t=0$, value is $0$.

  5. Exercise 5: Chain Rule (Two Parameters)

    If $w=e^{xy}$, $x=s+t$, $y=s-t$, then at $(s,t)=(1,2)$, $\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial s}=$

    Hint & Solution

    Hint: $w=e^{s^2-t^2}$, then differentiate by $s$.

    Solution: $\partial w/\partial s=2s\,e^{s^2-t^2}$, so at $(1,2)$: $2e^{-3}$.

  6. Exercise 6: Jacobian Entry

    For $F(x,y)=(x^2-y,xy)$, what is the $(1,2)$ entry of $J_F(1,2)$?

    Hint & Solution

    Hint: First row is $(2x,\,-1)$.

    Solution: $(1,2)$ entry is $-1$.

  7. Exercise 7: Critical Point Classification

    For $f(x,y)=x^3-3x+y^2$, classify $(-1,0)$.

    Hint & Solution

    Hint: Use $D=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2$.

    Solution: At $(-1,0)$, $D<0$, so it is a saddle point.

  8. Exercise 8: Lagrange Multipliers

    Max value of $f(x,y)=xy$ on $x^2+y^2=1$ is:

    Hint & Solution

    Hint: Critical points on unit circle satisfy $x=\pm y$.

    Solution: Maximum value is $1/2$ (minimum is $-1/2$).
