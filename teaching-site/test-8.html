Practice 8: Derivative Examples and Integration Preview

Estimated time: 40-50 minutes

Focus: Higher Math Lecture 8

Practice Mode

Work through the questions, open hints only when needed, then submit to check your score. Numeric answers accept decimals and expressions such as sqrt(2), pi, and exp(1).

  1. Practice 8 · 1

    For $f(x,y)=\ln(1+x^2+y^2)$ on the unit disk, the maximum possible value of $\|\nabla f\|$ is:

    Hint

    Write $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ and maximize $2r/(1+r^2)$ on $0\le r\le1$.

    Solution

    The maximum is $1$ at $r=1$.

  2. Practice 8 · 2

    The condition $xf_x+yf_y=0$ says the derivative is zero in which direction?

    Hint

    Look at the derivative of $f(tx,ty)$.

    Solution

    It is the radial direction from the origin.

  3. Practice 8 · 3

    For $u=x^2-y^2$, $v=2xy$, the expression $z_{xx}+z_{yy}$ becomes:

    Hint

    This is the lecture example for the Laplacian under this map.

    Solution

    $z_{xx}+z_{yy}=4(x^2+y^2)(f_{uu}+f_{vv})$.

  4. Practice 8 · 4

    If $z+xe^z+y^2-1=0$ and $z(0,0)=1$, compute $z_x(0,0)$.

    Hint

    Differentiate implicitly with respect to $x$. Enter a decimal or $-exp(1)$.

    Solution

    At $(0,0)$, $z_x+e=0$, so $z_x=-e$.

  5. Practice 8 · 5

    Compute $\int_0^1\int_0^1 (x+y)\,dy\,dx$.

    Hint

    The average of $x+y$ on the unit square is $1$.

    Solution

    The integral is $1$.

  6. Practice 8 · 6

    In polar coordinates, the area element $dA$ equals:

    Hint

    Area sectors widen as radius grows.

    Solution

    $dA=r\,dr\,d\theta$.
