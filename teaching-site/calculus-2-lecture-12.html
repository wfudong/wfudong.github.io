Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lecture, you should be able to:

  • Recognize when a line, surface, flux, or circulation integral is being used.
  • Apply Green's theorem in both circulation and flux form.
  • Use the divergence theorem to replace closed-surface flux by a triple integral.
  • Use Stokes' theorem to replace circulation around a space curve by curl flux through a surface.
  • Interpret the major vector-calculus theorems as one boundary-interior principle.
  • Define infinite series through partial sums and test the first model series.

Central Theme

The vector-calculus theorems say that a boundary integral can often be computed as an integral of a derivative over the region inside. The same accumulation mindset then leads naturally to infinite series.

Green's Theorem

For a positively oriented plane boundary \( \partial D \), Green's theorem gives

\[ \oint_{\partial D} P\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_D(Q_x-P_y)\,dA. \]

The flux form is

\[ \oint_{\partial D} F\cdot n\,ds=\iint_D \operatorname{div} F\,dA. \]

Divergence Theorem

For a closed surface \( \partial E \) with outward normal \( n \),

\[ \iint_{\partial E}F\cdot n\,dS=\iiint_E \operatorname{div}F\,dV. \]

This is usually the fastest route when the surface has several pieces or the divergence is simple.

Stokes' Theorem

For an oriented surface \( S \) with boundary \( \partial S \),

\[ \oint_{\partial S}F\cdot dr=\iint_S(\operatorname{curl}F)\cdot n\,dS. \]

The normal and boundary direction must match by the right-hand rule.

One Boundary-Interior Picture

Green, Gauss, and Stokes are not isolated tricks. They all have the same shape:

\[ \int_{\partial\Omega}\omega=\int_\Omega d\omega. \]

The practical question is which side is easier to compute.

Bridge to Infinite Series

A series \( \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \) is defined by its partial sums \( s_N=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n \). The lecture introduces the term test, geometric series, \(p\)-series, and first comparison ideas.
